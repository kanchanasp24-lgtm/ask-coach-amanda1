Sound familiar?

Do Any of These Sound Like What You’re Experiencing?

You don’t need to “try harder” on advice that wasn’t built for your body. If these sound familiar, your body is giving you clues that your metabolic health deserves a closer look.

Gaining weight — especially around your waist — even though you don’t feel like you’re eating much differently than you used to.
The things that used to help you lose weight have stopped working, leaving you wondering, “What changed?”
Experiencing an afternoon energy crash, feeling tired after meals, or relying on caffeine or something sweet to keep going.
Craving sugar or carbohydrates, feeling hungry shortly after eating, or wondering why you never seem completely satisfied.
Blood sugar, A1C, cholesterol, triglycerides, or blood pressure numbers have started moving in a concerning direction.
Frustrated because you’re genuinely trying to make healthy choices, but your body doesn’t seem to be responding.
Overwhelmed by conflicting advice on fasting, carbs, protein, supplements, hormones, and medications — unsure what actually makes sense for you.
Taking a prescription medication and quietly wondering what lifestyle tools can safely complement your care.
What if these aren’t separate problems? Your weight, waistline, hunger, cravings, energy, sleep, and blood sugar don’t operate independently. They are all clues about how well your body is managing and using energy — and that’s where metabolic flexibility begins.

The core idea

Health rarely comes down to one isolated factor.

Weight is not just about weight. Energy is not just about sleep. Cravings are not simply a lack of willpower. Blood sugar doesn’t exist independently from what you eat, how you move, how you sleep, how stressed you are, and what you take.

Most health advice hands you one piece at a time. Drink more water. Walk more. Eat less. Take this. What almost nobody does is sit down and look at how the pieces interact in your life — and then help you decide what deserves attention first.

That’s the whole job. Connect the dots before you overhaul.

Questions

The things people ask first

Will you tell me to stop taking my medications?

No. Starting, stopping or changing a prescription is a decision for you and your prescribing healthcare professional — never something that happens inside a coaching relationship.

What a pharmacist’s perspective does add: helping you understand why a medication may be used, what considerations could affect your health goals, and what questions are worth bringing to your next appointment. Many clients find they participate in those conversations with a lot more confidence.

Is this another diet?

No. There is no meal plan you follow until you fall off it. We look at meal composition, protein, fiber, carbohydrates, timing, hunger and satiety — and build eating patterns that hold up at a restaurant, on a work trip and during a stressful week.

I’m already taking medication for blood sugar, blood pressure or cholesterol. Is this still relevant?

Often, yes. Medication and lifestyle aren’t opposing sides. Coaching focuses on the factors you can influence day to day — nutrition, movement, sleep, stress, hydration, habits — alongside whatever your care team has prescribed, and helps you stay a better-informed participant in your own care.

Do I need lab work or a specific diagnosis to start?

No. Bring whatever health information you already have and are comfortable sharing. Coaching does not diagnose conditions or interpret labs as a medical service, and nothing here replaces appropriate medical evaluation.

How much time does this actually take?

Two 45-minute sessions and two 15-minute check-ins per month, plus message support. The daily work is deliberately small — the goal is one or two changes at a time that you can genuinely maintain, not fifteen you abandon by week three.

What happens on the strategy call?

A real conversation, not a pitch. Amanda reviews your assessment and application, asks about what you’ve already tried, what’s getting in the way, and what you want to be different in 90 days. By the end you’ll know whether this is a fit, what working together would look like, and what would be expected of you. If it isn’t a fit, she’ll say so and point you somewhere more useful.
